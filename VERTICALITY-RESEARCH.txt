YORU / 1.4 — VERTICAL CITY RESEARCH
31 October 2026. Sources consulted online; game rules are design interpretations.

HONG KONG: A NETWORK, NOT A COLLECTION OF OVERPASSES
The Hong Kong Highways Department describes the development of footbridges from
crossings into connected elevated and underground pedestrian systems. Its example
at Connaught Road matters because it links onward movement, rather than merely
landing on the opposite pavement. Hongkong Land's Central walkway account places
building-to-building connections at the origin of the network: hotel, shopping
arcade and later commercial developments function as connected destinations.
https://www.hyd.gov.hk/en/information_corner/hyd_factsheets/doc/e_Footbridges_and_Subways.pdf
https://hkis.org.hk/archive/materials/category/20161125122120.0.pdf

The Architectural Institute of Japan study of Central describes a network that
expanded incrementally both horizontally and vertically, through mixed uses and
public/private ownership. The design implication for Yoru is that a building can
have a genuine public concourse, with doors at a particular elevation. Painting a
highwalk through a module opens that passage; an ordinary private module remains
a destination rather than an automatic through-route.
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/79/705/79_2479/_article/-char/en

Hong Kong's Transport Department describes the Central–Mid-Levels system as a
combination of covered walkways, reversible escalators and travelators. It operates
as a sequence of transfers through a sloping city. Yoru adopts explicit transfer
landings and route costs. Escalator schedules and real Hong Kong topography are
not simulated in this release.
https://www.td.gov.hk/en/transport_in_hong_kong/pedestrians/hillside_escalator/index.html

LONDON: THE PODIUM BECOMES PUBLIC GROUND
The City of London's Barbican landscape guidance treats the podium and highwalks
as the principal public pedestrian environment. It also identifies wayfinding and
the complexity of routes as material issues. The Barbican's own access guide
separately identifies steps, lifts, ramps and highwalk connections. These are
operating differences, not interchangeable symbols.

Yoru therefore provides a floor plan for each stratum, persistent floor addresses,
labelled transfer destinations, a full-stack view and a cut above the active level.
The concrete slab is useful elevated land on which gardens, homes, markets and
workplaces can coexist. Pedestrians use highwalks and road pavements; goods streets
are a separate graph. A bridge crossing over another route does not create a node.
https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-Environment/spd-barbican-estate-listed-building-management-guidelines-volume-IV.pdf
https://www.barbican.org.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2024-06/Access%20Guide%20Feb%202024%20update%20v3.pdf

NEWCASTLE: STRUCTURE, STREET AND PUBLIC DECK TOGETHER
Historic England's MEA House listing describes a building suspended from roof
beams and concrete service cores, freeing space over the road. Its first-floor
public deck was designed to join Newcastle's wider elevated system; ramp and
motorway-crossing connections were added subsequently. The local authority's
heritage record also identifies the long ramp to the pedestrian deck.

This suggests two independent questions for the game: what holds a deck up, and
what makes it reachable? A supported but isolated floor is not a functioning
neighbourhood. Cores provide bearing, while stairs, lifts and ramps provide access.
Remove a foundation and the support path breaks. Close a transfer or cut power
to a lift and movement must reroute or fail.
https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1419279
https://sitelines.newcastle.gov.uk/node/46901

IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY
Thirteen strata, four-tile deck spans, connector capacities, electrical demands
and travel costs are fictional game parameters, not measurements or engineering
standards derived from these buildings. A stratum is a modular band of programmes,
not one literal residential storey. The game models bearing reach, route topology,
capacity and powered transfers; it does not solve structural loads, fire egress,
individual disability access, hydraulic pressure or a real city's regulations.
